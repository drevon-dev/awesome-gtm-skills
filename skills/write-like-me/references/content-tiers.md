# Content tiers, horizon, and hooks

Label every piece before drafting. The label decides what the draft needs from the library and what the writer should expect from it.

## The three tiers

**Commodity.** Ideas anyone in the topic could say and many have. "The key to losing weight is eating healthy." "Here's how to start a newsletter on Substack." Commodity content is not worthless. For a beginner, step one is the most useful thing you can give them. It is the tier that pays the writer least, because it adds almost nothing to their library and it is what readers detect as slop when it carries no person.

What it needs from the library: stances. Every topic has an infinite supply of commodity ideas, and the only choice that belongs to the writer is which ones they agree with. In personal finance, "skip the $3 coffee and buy the index fund" and "coffee money will never make you rich" are both commodity ideas. Picking the wrong one for this writer is the single most common cause of "this is slop". If `stances.md` does not settle it, ask.

**Personality.** The same idea carried by a specific person. "I run a $400,000 a year paid newsletter on Substack. Here's how to start one." Personality content with weaker credentials usually beats credible commodity content. A single mother of two writing about money outperforms an anonymous expert with better numbers. Founders do not need original thinking to build an audience. They need "I am a startup founder, and here is where I live, what I studied, what I tried, what broke."

What it needs from the library: at least one story or first-person detail from `stories.md`, and the writer's approved wording for it. Never invent one. If nothing fits, ask or leave a `[STORY: ...]` slot.

**Original.** A view the writer arrived at by thinking about one narrow topic for a long time. Pythagoras's six words. Cole's "language is open source". Original content is rare and cannot be generated on request. The skill's job here is to help the writer say a view they already hold as plainly as possible, and to reuse it once it is said well.

What it needs from the library: the writer's own crystallized line, banked in `approved-language.md`. If the brief asks for an original take the library does not contain, say so. Do not manufacture one.

## The I / you tell

Inside a sentence, "you should" leans commodity. "I think", "I did", "I never" leans personality. Use the person dial in `profile.md` as the baseline, and shift toward I when the label says personality.

## Horizon: timely to timeless

Every piece sits on a spectrum.

| | Timely | Timeless |
|---|---|---|
| Example | A meme on the day of an Apple announcement | An essay that took two weeks |
| Short-term value | High | Low |
| Long-term value | Falls off a cliff in 24 hours | Referenced for ten years |
| Markers | "just launched", "in 2026", "this week", a news peg | None; would read the same in 2030 |

Ask before writing: how much value will this extract, and over what horizon? Set the writer's expectations to match. Adding "in 2026" to a timeless piece trades a decade of reuse for a week of reach. Do it only when the brief asks for reach now.

## The three hook types

Same piece, three first sentences:

1. Commodity: "Here's how to start a newsletter on Substack."
2. Personality: "I run a $400,000 a year paid newsletter on Substack. Here's how to start one."
3. Timely: "Substack just launched X. I run a $400k newsletter. Here's how to start a Substack in 2026."

The hook sets the tier and the horizon in one sentence. Before writing a new hook, look for a banked one. Cole reused "When I was 17 years old, I was one of the highest ranked World of Warcraft players in North America" hundreds of times over a decade after it worked once. Repetition is how a line becomes owned.

## Labeling a brief

1. Which tier does the brief ask for, and which tier can the library support? Personality needs a story; original needs a banked view. Downgrade the label and say so if the library cannot support it.
2. Timely or timeless? Look for a news peg in the brief. Default to timeless.
3. Hook type follows from the two answers.
4. Write the label into the delivery note: for example "personality, timeless, personality hook (reused the Lisbon line)".

## Additive value

Every piece adds something to the writer's library. A restated commodity idea adds a point. A new story told well adds a lot. A crystallized view on a topic never written about before adds the most. When the writer asks what to write next, prefer the piece that adds the most.

## Brand versus founder

A brand account repeats the founder's chosen commodity ideas and stays out of the founder's life. Typeshare can post "five benefits of writing online". It cannot post the World of Warcraft line. A profile marked `kind: brand` therefore uses `stances.md` and `approved-language.md` and never `stories.md`. Greg Isenberg's extension: a brand can have five named humans behind it, each with their own stories, in which case each human gets their own voice folder.

## Volume

Under-publishing is the common failure. Volume builds association, and the library is the input that makes volume possible without repeating yourself by hand. The skill exists so that once a line or story is written well, the writer never has to write it again.
