# Learn interview

The samples do most of the work. The interview fills the gaps the samples leave: stances the writer has not written down and stories they allude to but never tell. Cole's observation is that everyone already runs a personal language model out loud, at every coffee and dinner. The interview gets those lines onto the page once so they never have to be typed again.

Rules:

- Mine the samples first. Never ask something a sample already answers.
- One round. At most five questions. Offer to skip any of them.
- Prefer questions with concrete choices over open questions. "Which of these two do you agree with" beats "what do you believe about X".
- Record answers in the writer's exact wording. The wording is the point.
- Ask nothing about credentials or achievements unless the writer raises them. Personality outperforms credibility, and the mundane counts.

## Stance questions

Pick the two or three commodity debates in the writer's topic that the samples do not settle. Present both sides and ask which.

Template: "In <topic>, people split on <debate>. Side A says <A>. Side B says <B>. Which is closer to you, and how would you say it?"

Examples for a founder writing about building with AI:

- "Ship fast and fix later" versus "slow down and get the architecture right"
- "Hire early" versus "stay solo as long as possible"
- "Cold outbound still works" versus "outbound is dead, build in public instead"
- "Vibe coding is real engineering" versus "vibe coding produces unmaintainable code"

For each answer, write an AGREE and a DISAGREE line in `stances.md`.

## Story questions

The unmade data set is how the writer became the writer. Ask for at most two.

- "What did you do before this, and what is the one sentence you use to explain it at a dinner?"
- "What is a mistake you made that you have told people about more than once?"
- "Where are you from, where do you live now, and did you move for a reason?"
- "Is there a line about yourself you have said out loud a hundred times but never written?" This one is Cole's World of Warcraft sentence. It is the highest-value question in the file.
- "What is a number from your own work you quote often?" (days to ship, bugs fixed, users, years)

Record each answer in `stories.md` with the approved wording as spoken.

## Voice self-report questions

Only if the samples are thin (fewer than three) or split in a way the writer needs to explain.

- "Here are three ways to say the same sentence. Which one is you?" Offer plain, elevated, and casual versions.
- "Which words would you never use?" Straight into `not-me.md`.
- "How do you sign off, if at all?"
- "When you disagree with someone in writing, do you soften it or say it flat?"

## What to do with the answers

1. Write the entries in the writer's wording.
2. Mark the source as `interview` and date it.
3. Show the writer the summary and ask for up to three corrections.
4. Log the session in `changelog.md`.

## Ongoing

After learn, the interview never repeats as a block. Instead, when a write request hits an OPEN stance or a missing story, ask that one question in the moment and file the answer. The library grows from use.
