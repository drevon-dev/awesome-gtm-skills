# Slop floor (fallback)

The full `no-ai-slop` skill ships inside this folder at `no-ai-slop/RULES.md` with its `eval.md`. Use that. This page is a one-screen summary for quick reference when drafting, and the fallback if the bundled copy is ever removed.

Slop, in Cole's terms, is commodity language nobody can attribute to a person. These patterns are the tells.

## Banned words

delve, foster, leverage, utilize, facilitate, empower, streamline, robust, cutting-edge, paradigm shift, game changer, this is huge, this changes everything, tapestry, realm, beacon, multifaceted, meticulous, intricate, paramount, transformative, elevate, embark, supercharge, harness, ever-evolving, seamless, unlock, navigate (figurative), landscape (figurative), testament, pivotal.

## Often-empty words and phrases

Cut unless they carry real emphasis or the writer's spoken rhythm: just, literally, honestly, simply, actually, truly, fundamentally, importantly. It's worth noting, at the end of the day, when it comes to, at its core, in today's world, the reality is, the truth is, in order to, going forward, let's dive in.

## Patterns

- **Binary contrasts.** "It's not X, it's Y." State Y.
- **Throat-clearing openers.** "Here's the thing," "Let me be clear." Cut and state the point.
- **Faux-insight setups.** "What nobody tells you," "the part everyone misses." Cut the setup.
- **Colon reveals.** A noun phrase, a colon, a dramatic reveal. Write a plain sentence.
- **Superficial analysis.** Trailing "highlighting", "underscoring", "showcasing" clauses. Replace with the actual consequence.
- **Importance puffery.** "Marks a pivotal moment," "stands as a testament." State the fact.
- **Interpretive metadiscourse.** "This matters more than it sounds," "as you can see." Delete.
- **Weasel attribution.** "Experts agree," "studies show." Name the source or cut.
- **Fake-strong verbs.** "Serves as a hub for." Use is, has, does.
- **Synonym cycling.** Repeat the clear word.
- **Negative listing.** "Not a X. Not a Y. A Z." Say Z.
- **Dramatic fragmentation.** "X. And Y. And Z." unless the writer's profile marks fragments as theirs.
- **Robotic rhythm.** Repeated sentence shapes and stacked punchy lines.
- **Rhetorical setups.** "What if I told you," "Plot twist:", self-answered questions, unless the profile marks question pivots as the writer's.
- **Fake-profound kickers.** Delete the closing metaphor. End on the last concrete sentence.
- **Summary-recap endings.** "In conclusion," "Ultimately." Cut.
- **Formatting slop.** Emoji in headings, bold mid-sentence for emphasis, bullets where prose reads better, headers over tiny sections.
- **Em dashes.** None in short copy. One or two in a long piece if they clearly beat a comma or period. Never as a rhythm crutch.

## The exception

A pattern the writer uses in their own samples, recorded in `profile.md` with a quoted example, is theirs. Fragments, question pivots, and anaphora are common examples. The floor removes AI habits, not the writer's.
